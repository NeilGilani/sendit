# Rider look research: modern freeride / park skier

Research for redesigning the Sendit rider (Quaternius humanoid, ~1.8 m): baggy shell clothing built from offset/inflated skinned geometry, plus a low-profile helmet and a large frameless goggle. Sources are cited inline. Where I could not find a published measurement, the number is an **estimate**, marked as such and derived from anthropometry plus the closest published figure.

**Takeaways for a 1.8 m rider.** On a 1.8 m man the back of the neck (C7) sits about 1.53 m above the ground. A 79–91 cm centre-back length therefore puts the hem at **0.62–0.74 m**: below the crotch (~0.84 m) and around mid-thigh. The look that reads as "modern freeride" is the long end of that range, roughly 85–90 cm CB, with a **dropped back hem** (the front 3–5 cm shorter). Width varies more than length between sizes. Norrøna told one reviewer that sizing down changes length by about 2 cm but width by about 6 cm ([Backcountry](https://Backcountry.com/norrona-lofoten-gore-tex-pro-anorak-mens)). So "baggy" mostly means extra girth.

Retailer definitions: **baggy** means extra-wide cuts, often with a deeper crotch and longer legs. **Loose** is a subtler version of the same idea ([Blue Tomato](https://www.blue-tomato.com/en-GB/l/baggy+snowwear-0000D48/)). Retailers frame the oversized silhouette as a 90s/2000s street style returning to parks. Snowboard buyers for F/W 25/26 describe loose, simple, old-school cuts as a staple rather than a fad ([Boardsport Source](https://www.boardsportsource.com/?p=141710)).

### 1.2 Construction details visible at game distance
- **Hood.** Helmet-compatible hoods are universal on freeride shells: Sabre StormHood ([arcteryx.com](https://arcteryx.com/us/en/shop/mens/sabre-jacket-0691)), and the Rush hood fits over Smith Vantage/Quantum helmets ([Halfway Anywhere](https://www.halfwayanywhere.com/gear/review/arcteryx-rush-jacket-review)). With the hood down it forms a **thick roll behind the neck** and a tall collar that reaches the chin. That bulk is the signature read.
- **Pockets.** One chest pocket plus two hand pockets plus a sleeve pocket (Sabre). The Rush has **chest pockets that stay reachable with a pack on**, and its hand pockets sit high to clear a hip belt ([PowderGuide](https://powderguide.com/en/magazine/materialtests/gear-reviews-arc-teryx-rush-jacket)). Visually this gives one or two long vertical or diagonal zips on the chest and high, angled hand-pocket zips.
- **Vents.** Pit zips on every shell listed (Sabre, Naikoon, 686 "Air-Flo"). Thigh vents on bibs (Sabre bib) ([Ski Essentials](https://www.skiessentials.com/products/arc-teryx-men-s-sabre-bib-x000008924)).
- **Seams.** Fully taped (Flylow, 686, Gore-Tex shells). On the outside this shows only as clean, flat seam lines. Multi-panel layout: shoulder yoke, side panels, articulated elbows ([arcteryx.com](https://arcteryx.com/us/en/shop/mens/sabre-jacket-0691)).
- **Fabric.** The Sabre went from an 80D to a **100D** 3-layer Gore-Tex ePE plain-weave face. The lofoten uses a 70D 3L Gore-Tex Pro ([Switchback](https://Switchbacktravel.com/reviews/norrona-lofoten-gore-tex-pro)). These heavy face fabrics are matte, with a faint sheen at grazing angles, and they hang stiffly in large planes and folds rather than draping.
- **Hem and cuffs.** Hem drawcord, powder skirt, Velcro cuff tabs, integrated goggle wipe (Sabre).

### 1.3 Bibs vs pants, boot stack, knees
- Bibs are now the default freeride choice. The Inertia credits the baggier trend for their rise in popularity ([The Inertia](https://www.theinertia.com/gear/flylow-baker-bibs-ski-snowboard-bibs-review/)).
- **Flylow Baker bib**: "freeride fit, articulated knee box", 1000D cuff and knee reinforcements, powder gaiters, a kangaroo chest pouch or two chest zips plus a snap pocket depending on year, adjustable suspenders ([evo](https://www.evo.com/products/250465-flylow-baker-bibs-men-s)). Blister calls the leg room generous and distinctly freeride, and says it runs large ([Blister](https://blisterreview.com/gear-reviews/flylow-baker-bib)). OutdoorGearLab mentions **large suspender buckles** ([OGL](https://offers.outdoorgearlab.com/reviews/snow-sports/ski-pants/flylow-gear-baker-bib)).
- **Arc'teryx Sabre bib**: built-in elastic **PowderCuff** gaiter over the boot, 100D Cordura cuff panel, Keprotec instep patch, thigh zips, RECCO ([evo](https://www.evo.com/products/225048-arc-teryx-sabre-bibs-men-s), [Ski Essentials](https://www.skiessentials.com/products/arc-teryx-men-s-sabre-bib-x000008924)).
- **Stack.** The cuff is wide enough to go over the boot shell and buckles. The extra inseam length bunches into two or three soft folds just above the boot cuff, and the hem drops to about the top of the second buckle. The reinforced hem band and instep patch are a different, darker material.
- **Knee.** Articulated knee box or darts. The leg is pre-bent, so the fabric hangs forward of the kneecap even when the rider stands straight.

### 1.4 Colour (2023–2026)
- Arc'teryx colourways seen on current Sabre/Sabre SV/Sabre bib: **Carob/Black/Dynasty** (FW25, 2026 tags), Black Sapphire, Forage, Azalea, Relic/Yukon, Stratus ([Snowleader](https://www.snowleader.com/en/sabre-bib-m-carob-black-dynasty-ARCT01519.html), [High Country Outfitters](https://highcountryoutfitters.com/products/sabre-jacket), [StockX](https://stockx.com/en-gb/arcteryx-sabre-sv-jacket-carob-black-dynasty)). Dynasty sits in the red/orange family and Carob is a deep brown. I could not find a colour called "Tui" in any source, so it is not used here.
- Trend reports: CLWR builds on black, off-white and dark olive with one seasonal accent ([Boardsport Source](https://www.boardsportsource.com/?p=151001)). Oakley 25/26 mixes bold earthy tones with cool misty shades, plus "uniform green" and a red accent ([Boardsport Source](https://www.boardsportsource.com/oakley-mens-outerwear-f-w-2025-26-preview)). Warm browns ("cocoa") and muted brights appear across categories ([Halfdays](https://www.halfdays.com/blogs/news/women-s-ski-fashion-2025-trends-colors-and-outfit-ideas)). Pink and black/white retro techwear are also noted ([Mountain Times](https://mountaintimes.info/?p=269371)).
- **Pattern to copy:** a dark or earthy base (olive, carob, tan, black), a tonal second panel colour, and one saturated accent on a small area such as zips, hood brim, a yoke stripe or the logo.
- **Logos:** small wordmark or icon on the left chest or upper left sleeve, sometimes a small mark on the back yoke. Loud oversized logos are a park/streetwear move, not a freeride one.

## 2. What the pros wear

- **Sam Anthamatten** and **Markus Eder** are The North Face athletes. Their kit is TNF Summit Series shells and bibs: colour-blocked, regular-to-roomy freeride cut ([TNF – Anthamatten](https://www.thenorthface.com/en-ie/athletes/sam-anthamatten), [TNF – Eder](https://www.thenorthface.com/de-de/athletes/markus-eder)).
- **Hedvig Wessel** (FWT champion) rides for **Peak Performance**, which became the FWT skiwear partner in 2016 and has extended its deal through 2031 ([inthesnow](https://www.inthesnow.com/peak-performance-extends-freeride-world-tour-sponsorship-into-the-2030s/), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peak_Performance_(clothing_brand))). FWT field kit is long Gore-Tex Pro shells, bibs and backpacks.
- **Candide Thovex** has his own label, CANDIDE: C1 3L jacket, C1 3L bib and C1 Vision goggles. Reviewers describe it as understated and timeless rather than flashy ([Snow Industry News](https://snowindustrynews.com/articles/candide-thovex-kicks-off-winter-with-new-outerwear-collection), [Mountain Weekly](https://mtnweekly.com/reviews/candide-thovax-c1-jacket/)).
- **Park**: Andri Ragettli is an ELHO ambassador ([Stupid Dope](https://stupiddope.com/?p=653477)). Alex Hall is a Faction athlete (skis) ([Faction](https://factionskis.com/de/blogs/news/the-collective-alex-hall-athlete-edit)). Park/slopestyle kit is the baggiest: long loose jackets or anoraks, wide straight-leg pants, often a hoodie or flannel layer. The Tanner Hall–era (mid-2000s) look took this to XXL extremes. That era is general knowledge; I found no citable source.
- **Common accessories** (consistent across freeride media; mitt reviews cited):
  - **Neck gaiter / balaclava pulled up to the goggle**: no skin between helmet, goggle and gaiter. Visible forehead between helmet and goggle is the mocked "gaper gap", and the fix is a brim flush with the goggle top, under about 6 mm of gap ([AMC Outdoors](https://oldprod.outdoors.org/?p=6389), [Outside Bozeman](https://outsidebozeman.com/culture/culture-more/lingo-etiquette/spot-the-gaper)).
  - **Leather mitts or gloves** in tan/brown cowhide or black goat leather with a long gauntlet over the jacket cuff, e.g. Hestra Army Leather Heli and Give'r ([OutdoorGearLab](https://outdoorgearlab.com/reviews/snow-sports/ski-gloves/hestra-army-leather-heli-ski-mitt), [Ski Mag](https://www.skimag.com/gear/warm-mittens-for-skiing/?scope=anon)).
  - **Backpack**: 25–35 L avalanche pack with a slim profile and ski carry. Optional, freeride only.

## 3. How games dress riders

- **Steep (Ubisoft Annecy, 2016)**: realistic freeride gear unlocked by progression. Jackets, pants, goggles and beanies, plus novelty costumes. Uses real-world brands and logos ([TV Tropes](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/Steep), [Horror Geek Life](https://www.horrorgeeklife.com/2016/11/16/steep-beta/)). The reference for Sendit's tone.
- **Riders Republic**: very wide customisation, from realistic to whimsical. Prada Linea Rossa collab outfits ([Twinfinite](https://twinfinite.net/2021/10/riders-republic-change-character-clothes-appearance-outfits/), [FashionNetwork](https://au.fashionnetwork.com/news/Prada-to-outfit-characters-in-Riders-Republic-videogame,1376346.html)).
- **SSX (2012)**: the art director dropped street clothes (t-shirts, jeans) for a "survival" layer of real mountain gear while keeping character personality ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2011-09-30-from-the-slopes-to-the-air-with-ssxs-wingsuits.html)). One critique was that riders became hard to tell apart ([Giant Bomb](https://giantbomb.com/user-reviews/uid/8959/ssx)). Lesson: realism needs strong colour blocking to keep riders distinct.
- **Shredders (2022)**: licensed real gear digitised down to small details, "authentic and stylized" ([GearJunkie](https://gearjunkie.com/?p=173903), [Gematsu](https://www.gematsu.com/2022/03/shredders-launches-march-17)).

**Readability at a 4–6 m chase camera** (my analysis, not sourced). With a ~60° vertical FOV at 5 m, the frame is about 5.8 m tall and the rider fills ~30% of screen height (~300 px at 1080p). Anything under about 2 cm in world space (stitching, small logos, zip teeth) is sub-pixel or aliasing. The silhouette therefore has to be carried by:
1. Volume about **1.2–1.4×** real baggy ease: the jacket must read as a separate shell over the body.
2. **Two or three big colour blocks** (yoke/shoulders vs body vs pants) and one small accent.
3. A **head read**: the helmet is the darkest or lightest mass, and the goggle is the brightest specular spot. The player reads facing direction from the goggle.
4. A dark **gap line** where jacket hem meets pants, and where pant cuff meets boot.
5. Rim/fresnel light on fabric edges so the matte shell separates from snow.

**Shape language.** The modern "tough, low-profile" helmet (Method, Obex, Ledge, Trooper) shares these traits:
- **Rounded-square plan view**: flatter sides, slightly squared front corners, a smooth crown with no peak.
- A **short integrated brim ridge**: a lip 10–20 mm thick across the forehead that sits flush on the goggle top, with one or two horizontal brow vents in it (goggle venting).
- **Elongated rectangular slots running front to back** on the crown, usually 2–3 per side in parallel rows, plus 2–4 exhaust slots low at the rear.
- **Rolled lower edge** that sweeps down behind the ear and rises at the nape.
- **Soft fabric ear pads** set into the shell's ear cut-out, flush to slightly proud.
- A **goggle clip** (rubber loop or plastic hook) centred at the rear at strap height.
- **Matte finish** is dominant: Uranium Black Matt, Granite Grey Matt, Apatite Navy Matt, fluorescent orange matt ([Viladomat](https://www.viladomat.com/en/men-s-ski-helmets/47701-1227742-obex-mips.html), [Snowleader](https://www.snowleader.com/en/obex-mips-fluorescent-orange-matt-POC_01153.html)). Gloss shows up as small accents only.

**Dimensions (estimate).** No maker publishes outer shell dimensions, and helmets are sized by head circumference: M is about 55–59 cm ([evo](https://www.evo.com/blogs/guides/ski-snowboard-helmet-size-fit)). A 57 cm head is roughly 0.155 m wide and 0.195 m long. EPS plus shell adds ~25–30 mm per side, so a size M low-profile helmet is about **0.21–0.22 m wide, 0.27–0.28 m long, and 0.15–0.16 m from the brow line to the top of the crown**. Bulkier resort helmets (Vantage) run 1–2 cm larger.

## 6. Visual spec for Sendit

All values are in metres for a **1.80 m rider** standing in a T/A-pose. "Offset" means the distance the shell surface stands off the **skin** surface along the vertex normal, after skinning. Values are **already exaggerated about 1.3×** over a real baggy fit for chase-camera readability (§3). Scale everything by `riderHeight / 1.8`.

### 6.1 Jacket (shell)
1. **Hem height:** back hem **0.64 m** above ground, front hem **0.68 m**, so the back drops 0.04 m (dropped tail). Centre-back length from C7 is ~0.89 m. *(In the game this read as knee-length over the stylised body; the shipped cut uses 0.83 front / 0.78 back, see character.md, "Jacket length".)*
2. **Offsets:**

| Location | Offset (m) |
|---|---|
| Shoulders / yoke | 0.030 |
| Chest | 0.045 |
| Waist | 0.065 (the jacket hangs straight from chest to hem; do not nip it in) |
| Hip | 0.070 |
| Hem | 0.075, with a slight 0.01 outward flare |
| Upper arm | 0.035 |
| Elbow | 0.040 |
| Wrist cuff | 0.045. Extend the sleeve **0.04 past the wrist** to cover the glove gauntlet |

3. **Hem edge:** add a 0.025 tall drawcord band with 0.005 inward pinch, two toggles at the side seams (0.015 cube, accent colour), and a dark AO line under the hem.
4. **Collar and hood:**
   - Standing collar rises **0.11 above the shoulder line** and closes at the chin/gaiter, offset 0.035 from the neck.
   - Stowed hood is a **0.06-thick roll** behind the neck, 0.18 wide, sitting on the upper back yoke.
   - Optional hood-up variant: hood offset 0.02 from the helmet surface, with a 0.03 stiffened brim lip framing the goggle.
5. **Panel/seam layout** (UV-space masks or vertex colours):
   - (a) Shoulder **yoke**, front and back, from the collar to **0.10 below the shoulder point** on the front and 0.14 on the back, in the panel colour, continuing down the top of the sleeve to the elbow (raglan feel).
   - (b) Side panels under the arm, 0.08 wide, in the panel colour.
   - (c) Elbow articulation darts: two curved seams.
   - (d) Centre-front zip line, 0.012 wide, dark, with a 0.03 storm-flap shadow.
   - (e) **Two chest zips**, 0.20 long, angled 10° outward from vertical, starting 0.06 either side of centre at sternum height.
   - (f) Hand-pocket zips, high and angled (0.17 long, 30°, at ~0.98 m).
   - (g) Pit-zip seam, 0.25 long, under the arm.
   - (h) Left-forearm pocket, 0.10.
   - Seams are 0.004 dark lines in the normal map plus a 0.015 taped band at roughness −0.1.
6. **Logo:** "Northwake" wordmark on the left chest (0.07 × 0.015), one icon on the upper left sleeve (0.04), and one wide wordmark on the back yoke (0.18 × 0.03). All in the accent colour or tonal.
7. **Accent placement:** zip pulls, hem toggles, collar inner lining (visible at the open collar), and one 0.02 stripe along the yoke seam.

### 6.2 Bib / pants
8. **Bib front** rises to **1.30 m** (mid-sternum), but the jacket covers it. The only visible parts are the suspenders (0.035 wide, with a 0.04 buckle) when the jacket is unzipped or in the lobby.
9. **Offsets:**

| Location | Offset (m) |
|---|---|
| Seat | 0.045 |
| Thigh | 0.050 |
| Knee | 0.045. Pre-bend the knee volume **0.015 forward** of the kneecap |
| Calf | 0.050 |
| Cuff | Ellipse **0.22 wide × 0.25 deep** (outer) around the boot cuff |

   For reference, the leg width at the knee is ~0.21 m and at the cuff ~0.22 m, so the leg reads as **straight and wide**, not tapered.
10. **Crotch:** drop the crotch **0.04 below anatomical** (baggy cut).
11. **Boot stack:** the hem ends **0.20 above ground**, covering the boot to roughly the second buckle. Above it, add **three stacked rings** between 0.22 and 0.34 m: each a +0.012 radial bulge, 0.035 tall, with dark creases between them.
12. **Cuff band:** 0.07 tall in a darker reinforcement colour, matte (roughness 0.9), plus an instep scuff patch.
13. **Seams:** knee darts (two curved seams per knee), an outer-thigh vent zip (0.30 long, 0.75–1.05 m height), and a cargo or thigh pocket flap (0.16 × 0.18) on one leg.

### 6.3 Fabric shading (Three.js `MeshPhysicalMaterial`)
14. **Shell:**

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| `roughness` | 0.82 |
| `metalness` | 0 |
| `sheen` | 0.3 |
| `sheenRoughness` | 0.6 |
| `sheenColor` | base colour lerped 40% to white |
| `clearcoat` | 0 |
| `specularIntensity` | 0.35 |

    Add a **fresnel rim** term `rim = pow(1 - N·V, 3) * 0.18`, tinted toward sky colour.

|---|---|---|
| Zips | 0.45 | darker than base by 40% |
