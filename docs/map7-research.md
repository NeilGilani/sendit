# Map 7 research: Fjordline (Norwegian ski-to-sea)

Background for Fjordline, map 7. The sources are text only: guidebook and route pages, tourism and operator pages,
trip reports, Wikipedia, Norwegian zoning plans and game reviews. No video was watched. **[calc]** marks numbers we
computed ourselves. **[est]** marks art or design estimates that no source states. Where sources disagree, the range
is given.

- **The range in one line:** Sunnmøre peaks reach "about 1,700 m above the sea" and average ~1,500 m, and you can ski

- **Romedalstinden:** no usable source found.

### Slope angles, couloirs, chokes, spines

- **Easy tours:** "many safe tours" stay at or below **30°** ([Arctic Norway](https://arcticnorway.com/discover/activities/skiing/ski-touring-in-the-lyngen-alps/)).
- **Couloirs:**
  - The Tomastind NW face is **45–50°** for its first 50 m ([search summary of downdays](https://downdays.eu/?p=159943)).
  - Øksehogget in Sunnmøre runs **45–50°** over ~580 m (1,900 ft) ([GearJunkie](https://gearjunkie.com/winter/norwegian-coulior-skiing-will-make-your-heart-race), fetch blocked; figure taken from the search snippet).
  - Store Trolltind (Romsdal) has a **500 m couloir at 50–55°** that ends in an icefall ([AAC](https://publications.americanalpineclub.org/articles/13201215386)).
- **Chokes:** Lyngen's Swift couloir has "tricky chokes towards the top" that have needed a rope. The Monster
  Couloir has a "tight, near-vertical choke halfway down" ([Mountainwatch](https://www.mountainwatch.com/Snow-news/the-monster-couloir-nikolai-schirmer-and-mark-abma-take-on-a-remote-line-in-norway-video)).
  **No source gives a choke width in metres.** We use **4–8 m chokes inside 10–25 m couloirs [est]**.
- **Spines:** Steep's Alaska update sells "spinelines, extreme crests, massive glaciers" ([Ubisoft](https://news.ubisoft.com/en-us/article/6vo723jG03BkbF9XwrZbXn/steep-free-alaska-update-now-available)).
  We found no Norwegian spine-angle data. AK-style spines run at about **45–55° [est]**.
- **Classic low-angle-to-the-fjord line:** Rørnestinden's North Couloir starts at a low col, "runs all the way to the
  base of the valley", and you can see the fjord while you turn ([Kyle Miller](https://www.whereiskylemiller.com/?p=31377)).
- **Mean face pitch:** ~1,300 m of drop over 2.5–3.5 km of horizontal gives **~20–27° on average [calc]**. The steep
  sections (35–50°) sit in the top third, and the run-out flattens onto moraine, birch scrub and beach.

### How lines finish

- "Every mountain here meets the sea and your last ski turns end on **snow-covered beaches**" ([57hours](https://57hours.com/review/lyngen-alps-skiing/)).
  Riders carve "right down to your sailboat" ([Mabey Ski](https://www.mabeyski.com/?p=7439)).
- Tours end "with an icy cold dip in the fjord" ([Arctic Norway](https://arcticnorway.com/discover/activities/skiing/ski-touring-in-the-lyngen-alps/)).
- Not every line ends on a beach. Little Skårasalen finishes on **scree with no landing stage**, so the group crossed
  to their boat by rubber dinghy ([Powderguide](https://powderguide.com/en/magazine/abenteuer-reisen/on-the-road-in-the-land-of-the-fjords-norway-part-ii)).
  Lower down, other lines become "a slalom through bushes in knee-deep slush", with hard avalanche cones at the bottom.
- **Tides:** Tromsø spring highs reach ~2.75 m. The spring range is **~2.6 m [calc from the tide table]**, which leaves
  a band of bare, dark, weedy rock below the snow line ([tide-forecast](https://www.tide-forecast.com/locations/Tromso-Norway/tides/latest)).

### Sea ice and the shoreline

- Warm Atlantic water keeps the large outer fjords ice-free. Sea ice forms in the **inner basins and small fjord
  branches**, helped by a brackish surface layer from river runoff. Across seven northern fjords over three winters,
  ice thickness ran from **0 to 0.8 m** ([O'Sadnick et al. 2022, UiT](https://munin.uit.no/handle/10037/26915),
  [IMR](https://imr.brage.unit.no/imr-xmlui/handle/11250/2719597)).
- For Fjordline this means open blue water in the main fjord and a strip of **cracked shore ice and ice-foot** in the
  sheltered cove by the village. That is plausible in March and April, and gone by May.

### Season and snow near the sea

- **Season:** Lyngen runs February/January–May, and into June in big years. Sunnmøre runs February–early May
  ([Arctic Norway](https://arcticnorway.com/discover/activities/skiing/ski-touring-in-the-lyngen-alps/),
  [Visit Norway](https://www.visitnorway.com/places-to-go/fjord-norway/ski-touring/sunnmore-hjorundfjord)).
  Couloir-week trips run in March and April ([Explore-Share](https://www.explore-share.com/trip/couloir-skiing-week-in-the-lyngen-alps)).
- **Snow:**
  - Powder is most likely in March and April on high north aspects. **Corn** dominates from late April to June
    ([Powderhounds](https://www.powderhounds.com/Europe/Travel-Services/Tour-Operators/Aurora-Backcountry/Lyngen-Alps-Private-Guiding.aspx)).
  - It is mild at sea level and colder higher up, and rarely below −10 °C ([Arctic Norway](https://arcticnorway.com/discover/activities/skiing/ski-touring-in-the-lyngen-alps/)).
  - Summits are strongly wind-shaped, with rain crusts under fresh snow ([Powderguide report](https://powderguide.com/en/conditions-report/snow-report-northern-norway-04-11-2017)).
- **Elevation bands for art:** wind-scoured, rocky summits; smooth powder or corn in the couloirs; wet, grey-white
  slush and **bare patches** in the bottom 100–200 m. Sunnmøre lower slopes have bushes and birch.

- **Midnight sun:** Tromsø, 20 May–22 July. Polar night: 27 Nov–15 Jan ([yr.no](https://hjelp.yr.no/hc/en-us/articles/360008027114-Polar-nights-and-midnight-sun)).
- **The sun never gets high.** Even the noon maximum is only 25–41°. **Golden hour (sun 2–12°)** falls around
  **18:30–20:30 in mid-April at bearing 265–295° (WNW)**. In May it moves to **20:00–23:00 at 295–325° (NW)**, and at
  midnight the sun grazes due north.
- **The light lasts for hours.** In late April and early May in Lofoten, "sunset and sunrise are connected in one
  magnificent show of colour and light lasting from 8 to 12 hours" ([Audubon / Sørgje](https://audubon.org/news/video-beautiful-time-lapse-photography-arctic-light)).
  Golden hour in the midnight-sun season runs from about 22:00 to 04:00 ([PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2018/06/11/forget-golden-hour-the-arctic-circle-has-golden-days)).
- **Late-April skiing:** north faces can be skied in evening sun ([Arctic Norway](https://arcticnorway.com/discover/activities/skiing/ski-touring-in-the-lyngen-alps/)).
  In a game, that means the sun can light the couloir from behind or from the side, not only from the front.
- **Alpenglow** is a pink-red band on the snow peaks opposite the sun. It runs through a continuum: sunset, then
  alpenglow, then blue hour "when the red leaches from the scene" ([Light Stalking](https://www.lightstalking.com/alpenglow/),
  [Meandering Wild](https://meanderingwild.com/alpenglow/)).
- **Sea smoke (frostrøyk):**
  - It needs air **~10 °C colder than the water**, so it is a winter and early-March effect.
  - It is usually low, so ship lookouts see over it, but columns can reach **20–30 m** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_smoke),
    [The Local](https://www.thelocal.no/20240118/norwegian-word-of-the-day-frostroyk)).
  - It stays over the water and almost never forms on land.
  - For golden-hour April use a **thin, low (5–15 m) haze over the fjord** [est] rather than true sea smoke.

## 3. Coastal village architecture

- **Rorbu (fisherman's cabin):**
  - King Øystein commissioned the first ones in about 1120 for the Lofoten winter cod season ([Arctic Norway](https://arcticnorway.com/discover/coastal-culture/origin-of-the-rorbu-cabin/)).
  - Built "on land, but with the one end on poles in the water" so boats tie up alongside ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rorbu)).
  - Two rooms: gear and food storage, plus a living room with bunks. One cabin could house up to 16 fishermen
    ([Baltic Travel](https://baltictravelcompany.com/attractions/rorbu-what-is-it-where-do-you-find-it-/)).
  - Older ones were log-built (*laft*) ([Hurtigruten](https://www.hurtigruten.com/nb-no/inspirasjon/severdigheter-langs-kysten/rorbu)).
  - **Colour:** red fish-oil paint was the cheapest, and ochre/yellow "gradually gained in popularity". Original
    roofs were **turf (sod)** ([Arctic Norway](https://arcticnorway.com/discover/coastal-culture/origin-of-the-rorbu-cabin/),
    [Full Suitcase](https://fullsuitcase.com/rorbuer-lofoten-norway/)).
  - **No source gives dimensions.** We use a footprint of **6×8 to 7×10 m**, a ridge height of 6–7 m, a pitch of
    **35–40°**, and stilts **1.5–3 m** above high water (taller on the sea end) [est].
- **Naust (boathouse) and sjøhus (sea house):**
  - Zoning plans give hard numbers. Pitched roofs at **30–40°** (Farsund, Narvik). Footprint capped at **40 m²**
    (Sula) or **68 m²** (Narvik). **Ridge height 5 m and eaves 2.5 m** ([Farsund](https://farsund.kommune.no/_f/p1/ib23dde42-d461-459b-be03-dc501be1492d/bestemmelser-oyvoll-rev-etter-1-gangs-behandling.pdf),
    [Narvik](https://oldweb.narvik.kommune.no/_f/p28/i29ab1f08-ac7b-45f7-bfb1-be70a2a09e64/reguleringsbestemmelser.pdf),
    [Sula](https://www.sula.kommune.no/_f/p1/ifda32f27-789e-4cec-a099-5d201706f2e8/2016-foresegner-for-nausthaugen-gnr-64-bnr-58-mfl-i-sula.pdf)).
  - Exposed coasts get lower pitches with small overhangs. Sheltered, snowy fjords get steeper pitches with long
    ridges ([Abaton](https://abaton.es/en/boat-house-coastal-architecture-in-norway/)).
  - A naust has a symmetrical gable with big doors facing the sea, and its gable end points at the water.
- **Colours:**
  - Falu/red-ochre paint is **#801818** (variants #7B1818–#81181C). It ranges from almost black to bright red
    depending on how the oxide was burnt ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/Falu_red),
    [htmlcolorcodes](https://htmlcolorcodes.com/colors/falu-red)).
  - Yellow ochre about `#C9962E`, white trim `#F2EFE6` [est].
- **Roofs [est]:** turf (green-brown, with snow drifted on it), slate, black glazed tile, or grey and red corrugated
  steel on working buildings. Snow load lies **30–80 cm deep** on the roofs.
- **Quays:**
  - Timber *brygge* (wharves) along the sea edge, each with its own rack ([UiT Foldvik](https://arkitekturguide.uit.no/items/show/956)).
  - Rorbuer often stand above a brygge ([Hurtigruten](https://www.hurtigruten.com/nb-no/inspirasjon/severdigheter-langs-kysten/rorbu)).
  - Stone moles / breakwaters [est]: a dry-stone rubble mound with a flat top **3–5 m wide**, closing a small harbour.
- **Boats:**
  - Nordland boats are clinker-built, **5–15 m** ([SNL](https://snl.no/nordlandsb%C3%A5t)).
  - The **færing** is a four-oared double-ender, **4.3–6 m** ([WoodenBoat](https://www.woodenboat.com/online-exclusives/faerings-and-femborings),
    [HRSMS](https://www.hrsms.org/model/faering/)).
  - The **fembøring** is **12.5–14.6 m**, with a square sail ([ScienceNorway](https://sciencenorway.no/history-ships/the-nordland-boat-is-the-legacy-of-the-viking-ships-we-were-probably-close-to-death-many-many-times/2221978)).
  - The **sjark** is a small motor fishing boat of about **10 m** (weak source: [babelfree](https://babelfree.com/dictionary/en/meaning-of-sjark)).
- **Hjell (stockfish drying rack):**
  - Built from two heavy A-frame trestles (*marstokker*) dug into the ground, beams (*æser*) across them, and loose
    young-spruce poles (*rær*) for the fish. Long diagonal braces ([Lokalhistoriewiki via search](https://lokalhistoriewiki.no/wiki/Hjell),
    [Wikipedia: Fish flake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish_flake)).
  - They stand on rock outcrops and beaches. Fish hang from **February to May**, while snow still lies on the ground
    ([Urth](https://magazine.urth.co/articles/the-fish-that-built-lofoten)).
  - We use top poles at **3–5 m**, bays **3–4 m** wide, racks **20–60 m** long, and hanging fish forming dark brown
    `#5A4636` curtains [est].
- **Lighthouses:**
  - Northern Norwegian towers are often **cast iron, red with one white band**: Tranøy **27 m**, Måløy–Skarholmen
    **34 m**, Landegode ([Wikipedia: Tranøy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tran%C3%B8y_Lighthouse),
    [Måløy–Skarholmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A5l%C3%B8y%E2%80%93Skarholmen_Lighthouse),
    [Hurtigruten](https://www.hurtigruten.com/en/explore-norway/history-culture/lighthouses)).
  - Smaller harbour lights (*lykter*) are 4–8 m white or red cylinders on rocks [est].

## 4. How games give mountains an identity

- **Steep (Ubisoft Annecy):**
  - Major landmarks such as Mont Blanc were **hand-shaped**, and the rest of the range was generated procedurally to
    rules.
  - The team studied geology so the terrain would "feel like the Alps". It is "not an exact re-creation… we wanted
    to make sure it was a fun place to play".
  - Designers walked the mountain to "find the perfect challenge on every face".
  - Drop zones let players go anywhere, and helicopter transit was deliberately cut ([GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/how-ubisoft-built-a-mountain-and-then-created-a-video-game-around-it/)).
  - Alaska is built around Denali and sold on spinelines, crests and glaciers, plus one Mountain Story per map
    ([Ubisoft](https://news.ubisoft.com/en-us/article/6vo723jG03BkbF9XwrZbXn/steep-free-alaska-update-now-available),
    [DualShockers](https://www.dualshockers.com/steep-title-update-5-and-alaska/)).
- **Riders Republic:**
  - Seven US parks are stitched into ~250 km²: Mammoth, Yosemite, Bryce, Zion, Canyonlands, Grand Teton, Sequoia.
  - Each region has its own flora, surface (ice, snow, dust, mud, stone) and player density. Grand Teton is kept
    emptier.
  - Beauty spots and "memorable landmarks" deliver the sense of place, and abandoned relics serve as curiosity hooks
    ([Ubisoft dev blog](https://www.ubisoft.com/en-au/game/riders-republic/news-updates/5I9IJDdoVEpxAPpdTcamo1/building-the-world-of-riders-republic),
    [GINX](https://www.ginx.tv/en/how-big-is-riders-republic-s-map)).
  - Criticism: abrupt biome seams ("hot desert meets snowy grass").
- **SSX (2012):**
  - 9–13 real ranges from NASA data, with features added by the "Mountain Man" tool ([Guinness](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/99143-most-real-life-mountain-ranges-in-a-videogame)).
  - Each **Deadly Descent gives a range one signature hazard and one piece of gear**: oxygen in the Himalayas, armour
    and trees in the Rockies, plus headlamps, ice axes and wingsuits ([Giant Bomb](https://giantbomb.com/wiki/Games/SSX_2012),
    [EA](https://www.ea.com/en-gb/news/ea-sports-ssx-game-changers-deadlier-descents)).
- **SSX Tricky:**
  - Courses are themed set pieces: a Manhattan downtown in Merqury City, a rail "bobsled" over Tokyo Megaplex, a
    cliff-face Alaska.
  - Garibaldi has several paths to one finish, while the original SSX had "all roads to Rome" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SSX_Tricky),
    [GameSpot preview](https://gamespot.com/articles/ssx-tricky-preview/1100-2801239/)).
- **Shredders:** seven areas, each with distinct terrain. Players move between them by chairlift and drone
  ([Press Start](https://press-start.com.au/?p=134166)).
- **The "weenie" (Disneyland castle):**
  - A landmark visible from the entrance pulls people forward. Use "picture spots", draw players "geographically
    and visually", and make the main path the brightest thing on screen (squint test) ([Boing Boing / Scott Rogers](https://boingboing.net/2009/03/27/gamedesign-lessons-f.html/amp),
    [Above the Garage](https://www.above-the-garage.com/wienies/)).
  - Hiding a landmark and then revealing it closer makes progress tangible ([Tancoque](https://tancoque.design.blog/)).

**Design rules we take from this:**

1. **Hand-shape the hero landmark and generate the rest.** Steep did it with Mont Blanc. Our hero landmarks are the
   summit pyramid and the village.
2. **Keep one goal in view.** The fjord and red village should be visible from the summit and from most of the run,
   like a weenie. Use a ridge or couloir wall to hide it, then reveal it closer.
3. **Give the map one signature hazard and one signature feature,** as SSX's Deadly Descents do. Ours are the couloir
   choke (hazard) and the rooftop line into the sea (feature).
4. **Fix the palette to one time of day.** Warm key light, blue shadows and red buildings make a screenshot
   recognisable at thumbnail size.
5. **Change the surface by elevation band** (Riders Republic): wind crust and rock, then couloir powder, then corn,
   then slush and bare patches, then beach, ice and timber.
6. **Avoid abrupt biome seams.** Blend snow into scree into shore over 50–150 m.
7. **Offer several lines to one finish** (Garibaldi), with a hard couloir, an open spine face and a mellow shoulder.
8. **Build picture spots.** Pause-worthy views at the col, the choke exit and the village drop-in.
9. **Use curiosity hooks:** a hjell, a lone naust or a beached færing off-line, as relics that reward exploring.
10. **Brightest-path test:** the sunlit couloir and glitter path should read as the brightest band on screen.

## 5. Design takeaways for Fjordline

- **Summit ~1,450 m above the fjord**, inside the real 1,290–1,560 m band (Sorbmegaisa, Slogen, Skårasalen). Average
  pitch ~22–25° over ~3 km horizontal, so the full run is 1.2–1.4 km of drop.
- **Summit pyramid:** a Slogen-style triangular silhouette, the global weenie, visible from the village. The top
  50–80 m is rocky and wind-scoured, and the drop-in is under a cornice.
- **Hero couloir: 45–50°** over 400–600 m (like Øksehogget and Tomastind). It is 12–20 m wide with a **6–8 m choke**
  two-thirds of the way down, and fans into an apron at ~30°.
- **Spine face:** 4–6 parallel spines at **45–50°** with 3–5 m deep sluff runnels between them, beside the couloir.
- **Mellow shoulder line:** 25–30° (the "safe tour" angle) for new players, ending at the same village.
- **Bottom 150 m:** ~15–20°, corn and slush with birch scrub and bare rock patches. It then narrows into a snowy
  beach and the village.
- **Village:**
  - 8–14 buildings: rorbuer **6×8–7×10 m on 1.5–3 m stilts**, naust **≤40–68 m², ridge ≤5 m**.
  - Roof pitch **35–40°** (30–45° range), snow-loaded, so the roofs can be ridden. Gable ends face the sea.
  - Colours: falu red **#801818**, some yellow ochre `#C9962E`, white trim, turf and corrugated roofs.
- **Harbour:**
  - A timber quay at 1–1.5 m above the waterline, plus a stone mole 3–5 m wide.
  - 2–3 færinger (4.5–6 m) and one sjark (~10 m).
  - A hjell 3–5 m tall and 30–50 m long, used as a jump or rail.
  - A red cast-iron lighthouse with a white band, about 15–25 m tall (scaled down from 27–34 m), on the headland.
- **Shoreline:** a 2–3 m dark tidal band of wet rock and weed, a cracked ice-foot and floes in the sheltered cove,
  and open navy water beyond.
- **Finish gate at the water's edge,** on the quay end or the beach. The fjord is visible for most of the run.
- **Sun:** elevation **~8–12° at bearing ~270–295°** (mid-April, 18:30–19:30 local). It is low and warm from the
  WNW, lighting west-facing spines gold. East faces glow alpenglow pink, and couloir walls fall into blue shadow.
  Never above ~25°.
- **Fjord water:** deep `#163A57`, a glitter path toward the sun bearing, and teal shallows `#2F5E5A`. Add low haze
  of 5–15 m over the water, not full frostrøyk.
- **Sky:** zenith `#3E6FA8` to a peach horizon `#F6C99A` on the sun side, with a pink band on the opposite horizon.
- **Season:** mid-April. Corn snow lower down, powder in north-facing couloirs, fish on the hjell, shore ice
  remnants, and no midnight sun yet.
